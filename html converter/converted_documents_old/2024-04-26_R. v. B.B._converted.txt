The Presence of Inadvertent Tainting [ 23 ] Inadvertent tainting impacts a witness’ reliability and is succinctly described by Justice Nordheimer of the Ontario Court of Appeal at paragraph 28 of G.(C.): ““inadvertent collusion,” occurs when one witness discusses the events with another witness with the consequences that the evidence of one or both of them may be altered. Put another way, a witness’ evidence may be “inadvertently” impacted by the fact that they have heard the evidence of other witnesses which “can have the effect, whether consciously or unconsciously, of colouring and tailoring their descriptions of the impugned events”
[1] [ 24 ]

In my view, there is no reason why the impact of inadvertent tainting would be limited to discussions with witnesses only. Throughout her testimony, S.W. has admitted to speaking to her stepmother, her father, J.W., her counsellor, Constable Jesse Sell, and the Crown, prior to testifying before me for the first two days of this trial. As a 15-year-old teen, recalling events dating back to when she was 12, and having to repeat and discuss those details with these people, appears to have had an impact on her ability to reliably remember certain details. [ 25 ] As it relates to S.W.’s reliability, I find that there was inadvertent tainting of S.W.’s evidence that impacted her ability to recall the facts leading up to the alleged offences, specifically, as it relates to how it came to be that B.B. arrived at her home and had volunteered to babysit her and her brother. The question is what impact this inadvertent tainting has on S.W.’s reliability on certain aspects of her recollection. [ 26 ] I have considered the inconsistent responses offered by the complainant on the above factual points. I do not place much weight in the discrepancies as to why S.W.’s mother required childcare on the date of these alleged sexual assaults. Whether that need arose out of a need to go grocery shopping or to go out on a personal date or to traffic in illicit drugs, is of little relevance. These were adult decisions far outside the control of the child complainant on the date in question. [ 27 ] While I appreciate the need to test S.W.’s memory as a whole, and the potential impact this may have on her credibility and reliability, the extent of questioning in so far as how it came to be that B.B. had been permitted to babysit S.W. and her younger brother places little weight in my overall interpretation of her evidence in light of how she would perceive those circumstances as a child witness. [ 28 ] Whether B.B. was waiting in the driveway in his truck or arrived while S.W. was inside the home and how her younger brother was moved from her mother’s vehicle to their home while asleep relate primarily to S.W.’s reliability. Those factual aspects of S.W.’s evidence are ancillary to the core allegations of a sexual nature S.W. has made against B.B., and I accept this inconsistency to be an innocent by-product of the passage of time on an ancillary factual point. [ 29 ] Furthermore, it is of little consequence whether S.W.’s mother pulled her daughter aside to check with her if it was okay for B.B. to babysit her, which S.W. maintains did not happen. [ 30 ] It is also uncontradicted and I find as a fact, that the mother needed childcare, and the eventual outcome of that need was B.B. offering to fulfill that responsibility to babysit S.W. and her younger brother at their residence on the day of these allegations. [ 31 ] However, what these discrepancies in S.W.’s prior statement do provide me is reason to be cautious and aware of the possibility that the mother of the complainant had attempted to influence the testimony of a child prior to that child meeting with police to provide a video statement in relation to the current allegations. [ 32 ] In addition to inadvertent tainting, the Court heard evidence of advertent collusion, which will be discussed in more detail below. The suggestion that a parent may have told or influenced the evidence of a child complainant must be considered in my overall analysis of the evidence, with particular attention to how this might have impacted the veracity and reliability of S.W. ’s testimony in court throughout this trial. [ 33 ] I wish to be clear that my examination of S.W.’s evidence throughout this trial is assessed with this particular caution in mind. [ 34 ] Having considered these factors, I find that S.W.’s mother did not pull her aside and seek her approval in advance of this happening. [ 35 ] I conclude that it is inconsequential whether the complainant’s description of how it came to be that B.B. arrived at her home and ended up babysitting her on the day of these allegations is precisely aligned with that of J.W. or B.B. I need not make a finding of fact on this given that it is not in dispute what those arrangements eventually looked like leading up to the alleged offences. [ 36 ] I also need not determine precisely why S.W.’s mother required a babysitter on the day of the alleged incident. The specific reason for J.W.’s absence that evening in my determination of the facts is of limited probative value. Having said that, I acknowledge in J.W.’s own evidence, she admitted it was because she had to leave S.W. and her younger brother at home to sell illicit drugs in order to support her and her children financially. I wish to make it clear that the Court is not casting judgment on the mother’s choices in this regard. [ 37 ] I am satisfied that J.W. was in need of childcare on the date of the alleged incident and that B.B. had volunteered to babysit S.W. which presented an opportunity for him to commit these alleged offences. I am also satisfied that B.B.’s expectation was that J.W. would return a few hours later with C.E. from Espanola. [ 38 ] Additionally, I find that B.B. was ultimately given this childcare responsibility because of the pre-existing relationship of trust that had been established between C.E. and the accused with S.W., G.W., and their mother. J.W. and B.B. departing the home to purchase and consume cocaine [ 39 ] In cross-examination, Mr. Michel asked S.W. if she remembers her mother leaving her home with B.B. and returning with cocaine and alcohol. S.W. repeatedly denied that this took place and insisted that: i) B.B. had never left her residence after arriving there on March 24th, 2020; and ii) While B.B. was at her residence, he molested her. [ 40 ] When asked if she remembers her mother cooking the cocaine over the stove, S.W. insisted that this also never happened. Mr. Michel pursued this line of questioning to challenge the reliability of her memory going back some three and a half years. [ 41 ] My assessment of S.W.’s evidence on this factual point must include a recognition of the contextual reality of S.W.’s perception as a 12-year-old child at the time, and the subject-matter of drug use and her mother’s drug dealing that likely would not have been deliberately exposed to her by her mother and B.B. on the day in question. [ 42 ] The evidentiary record from each of the adult witnesses that testified in this trial, points to the fact that B.B. was actively engaged in the consumption of crack cocaine on the date of these alleged events, and that this drug habit may have contributed to his unexpected visit to S.W.’s home, in order to purchase this drug from her mother. At the time, J.W. had just had her two children, including S.W., returned to her by Kina Gbezhgomi, an Indigenous-based child protection agency, just four to five months prior. None of this would have impacted S.W.’s recollection of what actually transpired that evening because it is highly improbable that she was in the immediate vicinity of her mother and B.B. for each and every conversation the adults had regarding their pending drug transaction. [ 44 ] It makes little sense to me as how and why S.W. would have knowledge of this aspect of events on the date in question. There is no evidence before me that J.W. or B.B. told S.W. about this brief absence from the residence in furtherance of an illicit drug transaction. [ 45 ] Put another way, there is no evidentiary link that draws the complainant into any of her mother’s illegal drug trafficking activities with B.B. on the day or evening in question, and its probative value in so far as my credibility or reliability assessment on the complainant herself is if little value to this Court. [ 96 ] I have carefully reviewed the transcript of J.W.'s testimony in this trial. She candidly admitted to engaging in drug trafficking at the time it is alleged that B.B. sexually touched her 12-year-old daughter. J.W.'s evidence included many of her own personal struggles that touched upon financial setbacks, substance misuse, and the combination of these factors in how they impacted her ability to parent. [ 97 ] It is undoubtedly a very challenging time in J.W.'s life as a single mother of two young children. She had only had her children back in her care from Kina Gbezhgomi for three to four months before this alleged incident. She openly discussed her financial struggles as a justification for her need to sell drugs to support herself and her children. To her credit, she also exhibited many aspects of resilience and courage in turning her life around and achieving sobriety from substances today. [ 98 ] While I sympathize with J.W.'s personal journey, I must identify concerns I have with her evidence in this trial. S.W.'s own testimony speaks to times when her mother told her what to say to police in support of these allegations of a sexual nature brought against B.B. However, J.W. testified that she never once discussed details of these allegations with her daughter. She further asserts that she never told her daughter what to say to police and certainly did not discuss with her aspects of this case in order to have their stories match up. [ 99 ] I do not accept J.W.’s evidence on this point and prefer the evidence of S.W. as it relates to the existence of collusion between the two of them. I find that J.W. had repeatedly told S.W. what to say to police and was aware that some of her suggested information to S.W. was knowingly false or exaggerated. [ 100 ] I also find that J.W. did so while she had primary care and responsibility of S.W. and that at the time of these allegations, J.W. had a significant influence over her 12-year-old daughter. [ 101 ] In my own observations of J.W., she did not seem to appreciate the serious problem with lying to the police about why she left her children alone with B.B., namely that she was going out for a “girl’s night out” when in fact she was actively selling and delivering drugs in and around the Sudbury community. [ 102 ] J.W. readily admits to misleading the police in her video statement. Similarly, I find that she was more than willing to mislead the police in these allegations by explicitly telling her daughter, the complainant, what to say to them. [ 103 ] I find J.W.’s testimony to be highly problematic. To the extent that there is any conflict in the evidence of J.W. and S.W., I prefer the evidence of S.W. and reject J.W.'s testimony. [ 104 ] I do not accept J.W.’s answers as expressed to Mr. Michel in cross-examination when she denies telling S.W. what to say to the police. S.W. describes information her mother told her to tell police that appears to be closely connected to the very child protection agency that removed S.W. and G.W. from her care a few months prior to these allegations being brought to light. The purported threat made by B.B. would have resulted in J.W. suffering an adverse effect in the context of a parent who had recent legal proceedings with that same child protection agency, namely, Kina Gbezhgomi. [ 105 ] I also find as a fact that J.W. had told S.W. to tell the police that B.B. had forcefully lifted her shirt when he allegedly sucked the child’s breast while seated on the couch in the living room. I reject the evidence of J.W. when she claims to have never told S.W. what to say. [ 106 ] Additionally, I also found J.W.'s evidence unreliable as it relates to her recollection of the specific aspects of the afternoon an evening of March 24th through to the morning of March 25th, 2020. Her reliability as it relates to the events and times shifted throughout her testimony and changed notably from her prior video statement to police. While these errors may be inadvertent, they were frequent, and the cumulative effect causes me to conclude that J.W. was not a reliable or credible witness. Testimony from the Accused, B.B. Law and Analysis [ 171 ] B.B. is presumed innocent, and the burden falls solely on the Crown to prove guilt at the high standard of beyond a reasonable doubt. It is not enough for me to believe that he is probably or likely guilty. [ 172 ] B.B. chose to waive his right to silence, and he testified in his own defence. He denied sexually assaulting S.W. both during his evidence in chief and while cross-examined by the Crown. This requires me to assess his credibility in the context of the totality of evidence in this trial. [ 173 ] In assessing the evidence, I remind myself of the differences between credibility and reliability. Credibility relates to the honesty or veracity of a witness. Specifically, whether the witness is expressing themselves truthfully to the best of their abilities. [ 174 ] Reliability relates to the accuracy of the witness’ testimony. In determining this, I must consider their ability to accurately observe, recall and recount the events in issue. A credible witness may give unreliable evidence
[13]. Accordingly, there is a distinction between a finding of credibility and proof beyond a reasonable doubt. [14] [ 175 ] In assessing the credibility of a witness, I am to consider any inconsistencies in what a witness said at different times. Inconsistencies on minor matters or matters of detail are normal and do not generally affect the credibility of a witness. However, one or more inconsistencies involving material matters can justify concluding that the witness is neither credible nor reliable. In determining this, it is necessary to look to the totality of the inconsistencies. [15] [ 176 ] The principles set out by the Supreme Court of Canada in R. v. W.(D)
[16] provide a requisite framework in determining whether the

Crown has proven B.B. 's guilt beyond a reasonable doubt: 1. If I accept B.B.’s evidence I must acquit him; 2. If I do not believe B.B., but his evidence leaves me with a reasonable doubt on any essential element of the offence, I must acquit; 3. Finally, even if B.B.’s evidence does not leave me with a reasonable doubt, if after considering all the evidence that I do accept, I am not satisfied beyond a reasonable doubt of his guilt, I must acquit. [ 177 ] As the trier of fact, I may believe some, none, or all of the testimony of any witness, including that of an accused. [ 178 ] I must also remind myself that if, after a careful consideration of all of the evidence, I am unable to decide whom to believe, I must acquit. [17] [ 179 ]

In applying the test in W.(D), the totality of the evidence must be considered. This was discussed by Justice Campbell [as he then was], in R. v. E.M.W., 2009 NSPC 33, aff’d 2011 SCC 31 at para. 47: “...It is not only appropriate, but necessary for judges to consider all the sources of reasonable doubt. The sources may include the doubt left by the complainant's evidence, the doubt created by the evidence of the accused, the doubt found in any other evidence or the doubt arising from the combination of those sources.” Assessing the Evidence of Children [ 180 ] At trial, the complainant was between 15 to 16 years old but testified to events that she recalled happened when she was 12 years old. As it relates to child witnesses, I am mindful of the instructions by the Supreme Court of Canada in R. v. B.G., 1990 CanLII 7308 (SCC), [1990] 2 S.C.R. 30 at para. 48: “ …regarding the credibility of child witnesses, it seems to me that he was simply suggesting that the judiciary should take a commonsense approach when dealing with the testimony of young children and not impose the same exacting standard on [page55] them as it does on adults. However, this is not to say that the courts should not carefully assess the credibility of child witnesses and I do not read his reasons as suggesting that the standard of proof must be lowered when dealing with children as the appellants submit. Rather, he was expressing concern that a flaw, such as a contradiction, in a child's testimony should not be given the same effect as a similar flaw in the testimony of an adult. I think his concern is well founded and his comments entirely appropriate. While children may not be able to recount precise details and communicate the when and where of an event with exactitude, this does not mean that they have misconceived what happened to them and who did it. [emphasis added] In recent years we have adopted a much more benign attitude to children's evidence, lessening the strict standards of oath taking and corroboration, and I believe that this is a desirable development. The credibility of every witness who testifies before the courts must, of course, be carefully assessed but the standard of the "reasonable adult" is not necessarily appropriate in assessing the credibility of young children.” [ 181 ] Two years after the release of B.G., Madame Justice B. McLachlin, as she then was, in W.(R), [18] reminded us of two notable developments in the law affecting the analysis of evidence from children. [ 182 ] The first was to remove any notion that the evidence of children was inherently unreliable and must therefore be treated with special caution. As a result of this change in the law, the requirement that a child's evidence be corroborated has been removed from the Criminal Code. Justice McLaughlin made it clear at the end of paragraph 23 in W.(R), that “…if a court proceeds to discount a child's evidence automatically, without regard to the circumstances of the particular case, it will have fallen into error.” [ 183 ] The second notable change in the law towards the evidence of children was a clear understanding that it would be wrong to apply adult tests in assessing credibility to the evidence of children
[19]. Justice McLaughlin goes on to explain that children may experience the world differently from adults and that we should not be surprised that details important to adults such as time and place may be missing from the memory of a child. Trial judges are instructed to take a common-sense approach and to not apply the same exacting standard that we would with respect to adult witnesses. This was a reiteration of the Supreme Court's decision in B.G. two years prior. Collusion and Tainting Legally Defined [ 184 ] The testimony of S.W. includes references to J.W. telling her what to say when they discussed these allegations prior to reporting to police. I am alive to the direct implications this has on S.W.’s credibility and reliability with respect to the core facts connected to the four distinct acts of sexual touching and the viewing of pornography. [ 185 ] As discussed above, Justice Nordheimer in G.(C.) has provided us with clear definitions of what constitutes deliberate, or advertent collusion and unintentional, or inadvertent tainting. [20]

Both are applicable in this case at bar: “[28]

As I have intimated, the term "collusion" has been used to describe two different phenomena. The first is deliberate or "advertent collusion", that is, where witnesses get together and fashion their evidence in concert in order to appear to be reciting a consistent and reliable story. The other, commonly referred to as "inadvertent collusion", occurs where one witness discusses the events with another witness with the consequence that the evidence of one or both of them may be altered. Put another way, a witness' evidence may be "inadvertently" impacted by the fact that they have heard the evidence of other witnesses which "can have the effect, whether consciously or unconsciously, of colouring and tailoring their descriptions of the impugned events": R. v. B. (C.), 2003 CanLII 32894 (ON CA), [2003] O.J. No. 11, 171 C.C.C. (3d) 159 (C.A.), at para. 40. This point was made in R. v. F. (J.), 2003 CanLII 52166 (ON CA), [2003] O.J. No. 3241, 177 C.C.C. (3d) 1 (C.A.), where Feldman J.A. said, at para. 77: - The reliability of a witness's account can be undermined not only by deliberate collusion for the purpose of concocting evidence, but also by the influence of hearing other people's stories, which can tend to colour one's interpretation of personal events or reinforce a perception about which one had doubts or concerns. [29] While the issue of collusion most often arises when a court is considering the admissibility of similar fact evidence, it is an issue that has relevance to the evaluation of a witness' evidence in general. [30] As this case illustrates, it is unfortunate that the term "inadvertent collusion" has been coined. As I will explain, "advertent collusion" affects the credibility of evidence. "Inadvertent collusion", or accidental tainting, does not do so. It affects only the reliability of such evidence. As a result, an entirely different analysis is required in determining the impact that "inadvertent collusion" may have on the evidence in question. Yet the term "inadvertent collusion " obscures this because the term "collusion" connotes conspiracy, which is a credibility concern. It would be better if the term "inadvertent collusion" was avoided and replaced by the term "inadvertent tainting". Given that the term "inadvertent collusion" was used during the matter before us, I will continue to refer to "inadvertent collusion" but as I say, that is a term that would best be avoided going forward. [31] I will begin with advertent collusion. It is self-evident that this first form of collusion is particularly problematic. Deliberate collusion among witnesses will inevitably undermine the credibility of the evidence given. No court would be comfortable relying on evidence from witnesses who have gotten together and decided on what they are going to say when questioned under oath, at [page731] least not without independent corroborating evidence establishing that their evidence is reliable, notwithstanding the collusion. [32] The second form of collusion, "inadvertent collusion", is more difficult. The fact that one witness has heard what another witness will say, or for that matter has even discussed what another person's recollections were, does not mean that either witness is not telling the truth, or is not giving their independent recollection, or that their evidence has been tainted. Indeed, even where the evidence of one of the parties to the discussion is inadvertently affected by what another person has said, the account of that other person may not change. For example, in this case, it is possible that when, during the joint meeting with the Defence lawyer, the appellant's wife offered reasons why the appellant could not be guilty, the appellant did innocently incorporate those reasons into his testimony, but that does not mean that his wife's observations were inaccurate or that her testimony had changed as a result of their discussion. Nor does it necessarily follow that the appellant's testimony became unreliable. He may have recognized from his own knowledge the truth and importance of what his wife had observed. The key point is that, unlike advertent collusion which corrupts the evidence of all participants, where inadvertent collusion has occurred, a close examination is required to determine what impact that innocent sharing of information may have had on the evidence of each of the witnesses who is a party to the exchange. As Sopinka J. said in R. v. Burke, 1996 CanLII 229 (SCC), [1996] 1 S.C.R. 474, [1996] S.C.J. No. 27, at para. 45: Under this approach, the trier of fact is obliged to consider the reliability of the evidence having regard to all the circumstances, including the opportunities for collusion or collaboration to concoct the evidence and the possibility that these opportunities were used for such a purpose. (Emphasis in original)”

[ 233 ] Additionally, there are two materially inconsistent statements made by S.W. that do not appear to be attributed to advertent collusion with her mother or inadvertent tainting in conversations with others, including her mother. They are as follows: 1. S.W. told Defence Counsel during cross-examination that she did not talk to her mother about these allegations; 2. S.W. told the police that when she was sexually interfered with, she tried to scream, and that B.B. placed his hands on her mouth preventing her from being able to do so; [ 234 ] With respect to the first materially inconsistent statement, both J.W. and S.W. in their respective testimony denied discussing the details of these allegations with each other. It is evidently obvious that their responses to each of those simple questions from Mr. Michel are inaccurate. [ 235 ] S.W. in her evidence has openly admitted that J.W. told her what to say prior to providing her video statement to police and has testified to those details in this trial. For this reason, I interpret S.W.’s answer to this question to be qualified with the various aspects of her testimony where she openly admits to discussing these allegations with her mother prior to coming forward with these allegations and at a time in her life when she lived with J.W. and was under her influence. [ 236 ] With respect to S.W.’s prior video statement to police regarding B.B. covering her mouth every time she attempted to scream, throughout her cross-examination, S.W. maintained that she did not say anything or attempt to scream while B.B. lifted her shirt and sucked on her breasts in the bedroom. [ 237 ] She also agreed that she did not say anything when B.B. allegedly “French kissed” her and performed oral sex upon her. However, there is no evidence that identifies precisely when S.W. represented in her prior video statement that she attempted to scream during the broader chain of events where she claims to have been sexually assaulted in the bedroom and living room. [ 238 ] I am alert to the assertion that in S.W.’s video statement, her reference to B.B.’s hand covering her mouth while she purported to have screamed, was limited to when she attempted to scream throughout the alleged sexual touching in the bedroom. [35] [ 239 ]

When directly questioned by Mr. Michel, S.W. denied any attempt to scream while B.B. was sucking on her breasts in the bedroom: [36] “Q. Okay. So, let’s get to the conversations. While he’s doing things to your breasts, you don’t have – you don’t say anything to him, right? You’re not screaming, yelling? You said that in... A. No. Q....examination for discovery. You didn’t say anything... A. Yeah. Q....right? A. I didn’t say anything. Q. Okay. When he’s French kissing with you, you don’t say anything, right? A. No. Q. Okay. A. I don’t say anything. Q. Okay. And when he is doing things to your vagina, you don’t say anything again, right? A. Yeah, I don’t say anything. Q. Okay. Now, I’ll stop there. Now, why did you tell the police in your video that.... MR. MICHEL: And if I may have a moment, Your Honour? THE COURT: Sure. MR. MICHEL: Q. That every time you tried to scream, he covered your mouth? Because that - that’s not what you told us on the 26th of August. A. Yeah. Q. That.... A. Her responses included a reference to confusion as an explanation for this inconsistency, but the reasons for her expressed confusion were not explored by either counsel. The Impact of Collusion and Other Materially Inconsistent Statements from S.W. [ 241 ] I re-iterate that there is no evidence that speaks to precisely when S.W. was purported to have attempted to scream throughout the course of the four distinct acts of sexual interference on March 24, 2020, in her video statement to police. [ 242 ] As evidenced in the above excerpt, the evidence is quite the opposite, where S.W. explicitly denied screaming, which infers that when B.B. was sucking on her breasts, he did not cover her mouth. [ 243 ] This is not inconsistent with what she testified to before me on the first two days of this trial. I find that this suggested point of inconsistency in S.W.’s video statement is not made out. [ 244 ] Accordingly, I find that S.W.’s general admission to falsely stating in her video that B.B. covered her mouth when she attempted to scream is in no way anchored to her specific description of how and what B.B. did to her when he initially invited her into the room, told her where to sit, showed her pornography, lifted up her shirt to suck on her breasts, and discussed specific details connected to his own experience of child sexual abuse. [ 245 ] I am alert to the existence of advertent collusion. However, ultimately, I maintain that S.W. was a credible witness throughout this trial. When Mr. Michel or Ms. Whillans put to her that she had said certain things to police or at a Discovery that were untrue, she admitted this to be the case without hesitation, and expanded on why she felt influenced by her mother to say certain things that she knew to be inaccurate. [ 246 ] Due to the detailed manner in which S.W. was questioned by both lawyers, it is not difficult to identify what S.W. accepts as being “her story” of what B.B. did to her, and what areas of her prior evidence were exaggerated or falsely stated. [ 247 ] She testified in a straightforward and descriptive manner by readily admitting when she could not recall certain details, or when she “zoned out”, or lost feelings of sensations in her leg when it went numb due to the way in which B.B. had performed oral sex upon her. She corrected both lawyers if she felt there was a misstatement or inaccurate characterization or contextualization of what happened from her perspective. [ 248 ] As it relates to her reliability, and inadvertent tainting due to discussions of the incident with her mother, there is indeed the potential for unintended or unconscious colouring of her recollection of the events that are not sufficiently corroborated through other evidence. [37] [ 249 ] Her physical description of how B.B. performed oral sex upon her was the subject of vigorous questioning by both lawyers in the two days that S.W. testified. Having considered her evidence in this regard, I do not accept Mr. Michel’s submission that it would have been physically impossible for B.B. to have performed oral sex upon her in the way that she explained. [ 250 ] The way in which S.W. explained in yoga terms, “child’s pose” and positioning of her legs in relation to B.B. is completely reasonable. There is nothing on the evidentiary record before me that speaks to this being anatomically impossible. What is highly unlikely is that B.B. remained in this same position for the entirety of the duration when he performed oral sex upon S.W., some 20 minutes, in her mind. It is likely that B.B. initially commenced the act of oral sex with this positioning as described but then would have likely adjusted his positioning throughout the course of this sexual act. [ 251 ] I do not expect a 12-year-old girl to enunciate the specific details of precisely how a 54-year-old man, whom she trusted as her babysitter, removed her pants and licked her vagina over three years ago. It would also be unrealistic to expect a child of this age to know how long this act took, and precisely where the hands and legs of the perpetrator, or herself, to be specifically positioned throughout the course of this inherently highly invasive sexual act upon her. [ 252 ] In consideration of the legal principles in B.G. and W.(R) in relation to the assessment of credibility of child witnesses, to expect that level of recollection from a 15- or 16-year-old girl would turn back the clock in how our courts are expected to interpret and analyze the evidence of children in today’s justice system. [ 253 ] I am aware that at various points in her testimony, S.W. testified to “zoning out”, being frozen or in a state of shock and being unable to move. Most notably, when asked about what she was feeling when B.B. was licking her vagina, S.W. stated that her mind went blank, zoned out from all the sounds, and that she looked up at the ceiling at the little dog on the bed. She thought to herself, “…do something, like help me….get up and bark at the sky or something.” [ 254 ] S.W. elaborated that she couldn’t feel anything, mentally shut down but could feel what he was doing, but “I couldn’t feel anything within my body that wasn’t relevant to that.”
[38] These gaps in S.W.’s memory on those peripheral details are expected given the trauma associated with such an alleged interference with S.W.’s sexual integrity. [39] [ 255 ]

However, despite the credible manner in which S.W. testified where she could not recall the details surrounding the oral sex and “French-kissing”, I remain concerned about the impact of inadvertent tainting on S.W.’s reliability due to the numerous discussions she had with J.W. [ 256 ] I have already found that J.W. had told S.W. to tell the police that B.B. had forcefully lifted her shirt when he allegedly sucked the child’s breast while seated on the couch in the living room. In doing so, I have rejected the evidence of J.W. when she claims to have never told S.W. what to say at all. [ 257 ] Cumulatively, this is too high a risk to overcome considering the combined impact of advertent collusion and inadvertent tainting that might have influenced S.W.’s ability to recall these specific events. I am also mindful of the evidentiary record that does reveal that she had experienced “flashbacks” or nightmares rooted in these allegations. [ 258 ] As for independent evidence to corroborate the allegations of “French-kissing”, oral sex and second incident of S.W.’s breasts being touched in the living room, there are none. [ 259 ] The lawyers have admitted that there was a complete absence of DNA that was sourced back to B.B. in any of the clothing seized by the police. However, I consider this to be a neutral factor given the length of time from when it is alleged this offence occurred to when S.W. provided her clothing to police. [ 260 ] The Crown has a very high burden to meet. In my respectful view, any facts subsequent to the alleged sucking of S.W.’s breasts and conversation that B.B. and S.W. had at bedside regarding his own sexual victimization as a child, is particularly prone to this unconscious tailoring of S.W.’s recollection due to the lack of independent corroboration. [ 261 ] For these reasons, in consideration of the credibility and reliability of S.W. and J.W. in conjunction with evidence of collusion between them, I am left with a reasonable doubt on the following facts: 1. That B.B. had instructed S.W. to lay down on the bed and then “French-kissed” her; 2. That B.B. performed oral sex upon S.W. on the bed; 3. That B.B. sucked S.W.’s breasts while seated on the couch in the living room; [40] Did B.B. touch S.W.’s Breasts after making pornography available to her? [ 262 ] I turn now to the allegation that B.B. made available sexually explicit material to S.W. for the purposes of facilitating a sexual assault against her and whether he had actually sexually assaulted her by sucking on her breasts while they were seated on the side of the bed in the spare bedroom. [ 263 ] I remind myself that although I have made a finding of collusion, this does not automatically render S.W.’s evidence valueless. [41]

For ease of reference, I will reproduce paragraph 31 of G.(C.), where the Ontario Court of Appeal makes it clear that where there is advertent collusion, my analysis does not end there. The presence of independent corroborating evidence must still be considered: “N o court would be comfortable relying on evidence from witnesses who have gotten together and decided on what they are going to say when questioned under oath, at [page731] least not without independent corroborating evidence establishing that their evidence is reliable, notwithstanding the collusion.” [emphasis added]” [ 264 ]

Here, there are several facts that were expressed by S.W. that are corroborated by Mr. B.B. in his own testimony that supports the reliability of her evidence as it relates to the showing of the pornography and the alleged sucking of her breasts in the bedroom: 1. The topic of masturbation is discussed by both individuals; 2. Both individuals agree that at the moment of first verbal contact, S.W. was standing at or near the doorway entrance to the same bedroom; 3. The topic of B.B. being sexually victimized at a young age is raised by him and discussed by both individuals; 4. The general location on the same side of the bed is consistent as described by both individuals; 5. The presence of C.E.’s small dog, a chihuahua or shih tzu, during the interaction on the bed, is explained by both individuals; 6. The presence of B.B.’s cell phone and pornography visible on the phone are discussed by both individuals; 7. The general plan to eventually watch a horror movie with the name “Jigsaw” or “Saw” in the living room, is identical; 8. Both individuals testified that the interaction in the bedroom preceded the commencement of the horror movie being turned on in the living room; 9. Both individuals confirm that throughout this entire interaction in the bedroom, that G.W. was sleep and no one else was present upon J.W.’s departure; 10. J.W.’s absence from the home for several hours into the following morning is agreed to both individuals; [ 265 ] I have carefully reviewed S.W.’s testimony and considered each distinct description of the initial events in the bedroom as explained by B.B. There is in fact, independent, corroborating evidence as provided by B.B. himself as it relates to the initial encounter in this bedroom, and how it progressed to the point of him sucking on S.W.’s breasts, while they both sat on the side of the bed, and then discussed his own personal experience of being sexually abused at a young age. [ 266 ] I am satisfied beyond a reasonable doubt that any degree of advertent collusion with J.W. has not tainted S.W.’s credibility on these specific facts in the bedroom that include the showing of pornography, sucking of her breasts and subsequent discussion regarding B.B.’s personal experience as a victim of child sexual abuse. [ 267 ] I am also satisfied that the reliability of S.W.’s evidence as it relates to these same facts has not been tainted despite the potential for inadvertent collusion due to any discussions she may have had with J.W. [ 268 ] The similarities in the subject-matter, physical location, logistical organization of the room and the words uttered on very unusual topics of masturbation and B.B.’s own child sexual abuse experience, adds a strong layer of reliability to S.W.’s evidence that is compelling, even in the face of advertent collusion with J.W. detached from this particular line of factual inquiry. [ 269 ] I come to this conclusion because in a careful review of the specific areas that S.W.’s admits were a product of what J.W. told her to say, they do not detract away from the reliability in the way S.W. explained how she came to be situated in the bedroom or the details surrounding the showing of pornography and sucking of her breasts. Similarly, the conversation regarding B.B.’s own sexual victimization as a child is also not impacted by any advertent collusion with J.W. [ 270 ] S.W.’s evidence was firm and unshaken on the core underlying allegations that lead up to him sucking her breasts in the bedroom, through to the point when he apologized to her because he was molested as a child, while admitting this was a bad habit of his. [42] [ 271 ] The facts that point to B.B. initiating the topic of masturbation, showing her graphic pornography, lifting up her shirt and sucking on her breasts and specifically how the subject-matter of B.B. being sexually abused as a child came about remained consistent and thorough in S.W.’s testimony both in chief and during cross-examination. [ 272 ] S.W. testified that after her mother had departed for Espanola, she left her bedroom with the understanding that she was going to watch the horror movie, Jigsaw” with B.B. in the living room. [ 273 ] She reiterated that while she was situated in the hallway looking through the doorframe to the bedroom, B.B. had said to her, “I heard you masturbate.” S.W.’s response provided an age-appropriate response that spoke to her level of sexual immaturity on this topic. She replied by telling B.B. that she was “not a good fisher”, or that she was “not good at fishing”. [ 274 ] In a candid manner, she told the Court that she did not know what B.B. meant and that she had thought he was referring to fishing. [43] This prompted B.B. to repeat the same question about her masturbating, but S.W. testified she remained confused. [ 275 ] When cross-examined by Mr. Michel, S.W. maintained that B.B. had called her in to the bedroom and that when she entered, he was seated at the foot of the bed, not at the head of the bed as put to her by Defence Counsel. She also disagreed with counsel and maintained that there was no dresser inside that room. [ 276 ] She specified that she sat at B.B.’s left side, closer to the foot of the bed than him, at which point, he grabbed her right elbow, took out his cell phone and proceeded to log onto a pornography website in her presence. [44] She described this website similar to a Youtube, but for sex, and that B.B. would scroll down and in doing so, more images of people having sex would appear on his cell phone. [ 277 ] He then held the phone out in his hand for both of them to view and clicked on a number of pornography videos in her immediate view. S.W. testified that she was in a state of shock, experienced the sensation of going blank and zoned out while the videos were being played. [ 278 ] She recalls the end of the very last video when she zoned back in and was able to provide a graphic description of sexual intercourse involving a woman and two men. [45] B.B. then turned off his phone, put it away. [ 279 ]

While she was in a state of shock, B.B. lifted up S.W.’s shirt and began to suck on her breasts while she was seated on the side of the bed beside him. She specified that B.B. only lifted up her shirt partially just enough to expose her breasts. When B.B. was sucking her breasts, S.W. described it this way: [46] “A. I was still in shock. I - I couldn't move. I was like frozen in place. I - I couldn't move. I didn't scream. I didn't yell for help. I just kind of sat there, hoping it would end. Q. And when he had lifted up your shirt, were you still sitting at this time? A. Yes, I was still sitting.” [ 280 ] I would like to relate this back to my conclusions above on the prior inconsistent statement that S.W. had admitted in relation to claiming that each time she attempted to scream, that B.B. covered her mouth. As indicated above, while clearly inconsistent with her prior video statement, that line of questioning was not specified or anchored to specific attempts made by S.W. to scream throughout the course of the totality of the allegations. [ 281 ] Here, while B.B. proceeded to suck on S.W.’s breasts while she sat on the bed, it is clear to me that she made no such attempt to scream. Her evidence points to her being in a state of shock, frozen and unable to scream while B.B. showed her pornography and then sucked on her breasts. Accordingly, I do not see this particular aspect of S.W.’s evidence to be impacted in any way with the prior inconsistent statement I discussed above. [ 282 ] I find that B.B. had expressed to S.W. on more than one occasion that he heard she masturbates. I also find that he invited her into the bedroom and directed S.W. to sit at the foot of the bed at which point he deliberately showed graphic pornographic videos to S.W. on his cell phone for the purposes of sexually assaulting her. Immediately thereafter, he lifted up her shirt and sucked on her breasts while S.W. sat there frozen and unable to move due to shock. [ 283 ] He then went on to apologize to S.W. and then shared with her his own childhood victimization at the hands of a trusted adult, who sexually abused him, and justified this as a reason for the sexual interference that he had just committed upon S.W. [ 284 ] S.W.’s testimony in chief was significantly detailed and during cross-examination, she remained firm on how it came to be that B.B. had shown her pornography and sucked on her breasts in the bedroom. She also had no hesitation in correcting Mr. Michel on certain aspects of her evidence that she maintains are true and firmly disputed the aspects of her evidence that conflicted with B.B.’s version of events. [ 285 ] Her responses as to why she believed her mother was absent (grocery shopping) and instinctual reaction to the comments from B.B. regarding her masturbation (I’m not good at fishing), provide a layer of authenticity indicative of her youthful age and child-like mind set. [ 286 ] I find beyond a reasonable doubt that S.W.’s testimony explaining how B.B. invited her into the bedroom, showed her pornography and sucked her breasts, up to and including the discussion she had with B.B. regarding his own sexual victimization is significantly reliable in consideration of the independently corroborating facts in B.B.’s own evidence and other qualities within her testimony. [ 287 ] I am convinced beyond a reasonable doubt that the level of detail and certainty that S.W. provided in describing how B.B. invited her to sit down on the bed, showed her pornography, proceeded to suck her breasts after discussing masturbation with her is persuasive and untouched by any of the lies that she had mentioned in other aspects of her evidence in this trial, including the advertent collusion with J.W. [ 288 ] It is also clear to me that the only purpose behind B.B. making this graphic pornography available to S.W. was because he had settled in his mind that he was going to sexually assault her. Moments later, he did just that, and then apologized and attempted to justify it to her. [ 289 ] S.W. was cross-examined on various ancillary points that in my view, did little to weaken her credibility or reliability due to their ancillary nature in consideration of how Courts are to analyze the evidence of children. For example, it is not surprising to the Court that S.W. was unable to identify alcohol or drugs in the bedroom. We do not know what a 15- or 16-year-old teen would identify and interpret to meet the definition of those two substances. No details on what she in her own mind thought “drugs” or “alcohol” looked like from her youthful age were asked of her. [ 290 ] Although there are variances in the testimony of the witnesses in describing the specific location of the furniture arrangements in the bedroom, this does not in my view, detract away from the credibility and reliability of the central elements of S.W.’s testimony as it relates to the actual offences alleged. Some minor variances are expected on ancillary facts such as this given B.B.’s degree of impairment, the dated nature of these events and S.W.’s youthful age. [ 291 ] As I identified earlier, S.W.’s initial confusion and response to B.B.’s comments regarding masturbation (“I’m not good at fishing”), provide the Court with a persuasive layer of authenticity that is genuinely connected to her youthful age and child-like mind set. This strengthens the reliability of her testimony. [ 292 ] Despite her status as a child witness, her ability to articulate what happened to her was strong even in the face of lengthy questioning from both lawyers. I did not perceive S.W. to be evasive or attempt to avoid any aspects of her evidence that were admittedly untrue or materially inconsistent with what she had told the police. [ 293 ] In fact, I found S.W. to be open and forthright with those portions of her evidence that were quite determinantal to the Crown’s case. In the areas of her evidence where her mother told her to say certain things, she readily admitted to them and remained focused on the remaining details of what she described happened to her while maintaining their truthfulness. [ 294 ] Additionally, I am also mindful of the level of influence J.W. would have had on S.W. at the time the video statement was provided back in March 2020, and the fact that S.W.’s personal circumstances have changed. Today, she no longer lives with her mother and has had very little contact with her in over a year. The net impact of this change in S.W.’s life is that it assists me in assessing her testimony before me as evidently free of any influence or input from J.W. in this trial. [ 295 ] As discussed above, I remind myself that the unfolding of events as described by S.W. is corroborated to a significant degree by B.B.’s own testimony. Those facts that align between the testimony provided by S.W. and B.B., add a significant layer of reliability and credibility to her evidence, despite my findings as it relates to advertent collusion in these Reasons. [ 296 ] In a careful review of S.W.’s evidence in conjunction with the totality of the record, I am persuaded beyond a reasonable doubt and accept S.W.’s description of how her sexual integrity was violated by B.B. from the moment he arrived up to and including the showing of the pornography, him sucking on her breasts and the discussions that took place immediately afterwards regarding his own sexual victimization as a child. Conclusion [ 297 ] I remind myself of the Crown’s burden to prove each of the essential elements of the offences to the standard of beyond a reasonable doubt. In accordance with the principles in W.D., I make the following conclusions having considered the totality of the evidence and applicable legal principles: 1. I do not accept the evidence of B.B. 2. His evidence as presented in its entirety does not leave me with a reasonable doubt on any of the essential elements of the offences at Counts 1, 2 and 3. 3. After considering all of the evidence that I do accept, I am satisfied beyond a reasonable doubt that B.B. committed each of the offences on Counts 1 to 3 as charged. [ 298 ] Accordingly, there will be a finding of guilty on Count one on the Information, the offence of Sexual Assault contrary to section 271 of the Criminal Code. [ 299 ] I am also making a finding of guilty on Count three, the offence of Making Sexually Explicit Material Available to a Child Under the Age of 14, contrary to section 171.1(1) (b) of the Criminal Code. [ 300 ] Count two, which is the offence of Sexual Interference pursuant to section 151(a), is hereby stayed pursuant to the principles in R. v. Kienapple, 1974 CanLII 14 (SCC), [1975] 1 S.C.R. 729.
